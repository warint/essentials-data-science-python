The Roller Coaster Problem

There is a law of physics hiding in this spreadsheet. Find its exponent.

The brief

You have 84 roller coasters. For each one: the height of its first drop, its top speed, its length, how many inversions it has, and how long the queue was when somebody measured it.

Two of those columns are connected by a physical law of the form \(v = c\,h^{k}\). Your job is to report \(k\), with an interval, and to say what \(c\) turned out to be.

There is a specific right answer. It is a number you can look up in a physics textbook, and you will know when you have it. Do not look it up first — the point is to recover it from data.

Ninety minutes

00–15 Look, and fit the obvious model and write down its \(R^2\)
15–35 Diagnose it residuals, and what they show
35–60 Find the functional form and estimate the exponent
60–80 Find the rows in charge and decide what to do about them
80–90 Write it up 250 words

Start from starter/session01_starter.py. It loads the data and gets out of the way.

Stage 1 · Look, and fit — 15 min

import essentials as ess

d = ess.load("coasters")
  1. Plot top speed against drop height. Actually plot it, before fitting.
  2. Fit speed_kmh ~ drop_m and write down the coefficient in its units.
  3. Write down \(R^2\).

Keep that \(R^2\) written down somewhere. You are going to need it later, when it turns out to have been no help at all.

Stage 2 · Diagnose it — 20 min

  1. Plot residuals against fitted values.
  2. Say in a sentence what structure you can see. Not “looks fine” — name the shape, and name what you were looking for.
  3. Bin the residuals by drop height and take the mean in each bin. Does the sign of the mean residual change as you move across the range?
  4. Add a squared term and test it. What does the \(p\)-value say?

If your answer to 2 is that there is no structure, look again. There is, and it is not subtle once you know to look for a change of sign.

Stage 3 · Find the law — 25 min

You are looking for \(v = c\,h^{k}\). Taking logs of both sides gives you something you already know how to fit.

\[\log v = \log c + k \log h\]

The slope of that regression is the exponent. The exponential of the intercept is \(c\).

  1. Fit it. Report \(k\) with a 95% confidence interval.
  2. Report \(c\).
  3. Does your interval contain a simple fraction? Which one?
  4. Now say what physical statement that fraction corresponds to.

Stage 4 · Find the rows in charge — 20 min

inf = fit.get_influence()
leverage = inf.hat_matrix_diag
cooks    = inf.cooks_distance[0]
  1. Confirm that the leverages sum to \(p\). They do, exactly. Say why.
  2. Find the six observations with the largest Cook’s distance.
  3. Go and read those rows. Print them. What do they have in common?
  4. Re-estimate \(k\) with and without them. Report both numbers.
  5. Decide whether to keep them, and write down the argument — not the decision.

This stage carries the most marks. A group that can explain why those rows behave differently has understood the session; a group that deleted them because they were far from the line has not.

What you hand in

250 words, plus your script. It must contain:

  • The exponent, with an interval, and the constant
  • The physical law you recovered, stated in one sentence
  • The \(R^2\) of your first model, and one sentence on why it was misleading
  • The six rows, what they turned out to be, and your decision about them
  • One thing this analysis does not license you to claim

And the standing rule: one model output — from a language model, or from your own regression — that you identified as wrong, and how you established that.

What loses marks

  • Reporting a coefficient with no units
  • Reporting \(R^2\) with no diagnostic plot
  • “The residuals looked fine” with no statement of what you looked for
  • Deleting influential observations without naming them
  • Reporting the exponent as 0.37 — which is what you get if you keep all 84 rows, and which corresponds to no law of anything

And, everywhere in this course: an association described in causal language. Session 6 is where we earn the right to that verb.

Two-minute report

One number the exponent, with its interval
One diagnostic what you plotted, and what it showed
One decision the rows you set aside, and the argument for it

The presenter is drawn at random when your group is called. All of you prepare.
