The Mushroom Forager

Hand in a rule you would actually eat from.

The brief

1,400 mushrooms from one forest, each with nine measurable features and a verdict. Your job is a decision rule: given a mushroom, eat it or discard it.

The deliverable is not a model. It is a rule with a threshold, plus the two costs that justify that threshold, plus the number of poisonous mushrooms your rule would let through.

A rule that maximises accuracy will kill you. That is not a figure of speech about this dataset — it is the arithmetic, and finding it is stage 4.

Ninety minutes

00–10 The baseline the number every result is read against
10–30 Fit, and read it as odds ratios which feature dominates?
30–50 The confusion matrix at \(t = 0.5\), and what it costs
50–75 Choose a threshold from costs and defend it
75–85 The graded outcome ordinal, if you get there
85–90 Write it up 250 words

Stage 1 · The baseline — 10 min

import essentials as ess
d = ess.load("mushrooms")
  1. What share of mushrooms are poisonous?
  2. If you predicted “edible” every time, what would your accuracy be?
  3. How many poisonous mushrooms would that rule feed you?

Write that accuracy at the top of your script. Every accuracy figure you produce today has to be reported next to it, or it means nothing.

Stage 2 · Fit, and read — 20 min

import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
import numpy as np

fit = smf.logit("poisonous ~ ...", data=d).fit()
np.exp(fit.params)          # odds ratios
  1. Fit a logistic regression using all nine features. Treat the categorical ones with C(...).
  2. Report the coefficients as odds ratios, sorted.
  3. One feature dominates. Which, and by how much? Look it up afterwards — it has a name and a reason.
  4. Take that feature’s odds ratio and work out what it does to a mushroom at \(p = 0.01\), and to one at \(p = 0.90\). Explain the difference.

Stage 3 · The confusion matrix — 20 min

from sklearn.metrics import confusion_matrix, roc_auc_score

p_hat = fit.predict(d)                        # probabilities, not classes
confusion_matrix(d.poisonous, (p_hat > 0.5).astype(int))
  1. Build the matrix at \(t = 0.5\). Label the four cells in words.
  2. Report accuracy, and put the stage 1 baseline next to it.
  3. How many poisonous mushrooms did this rule call edible? Express it as one meal in how many.
  4. Report AUC. Say in one sentence why AUC cannot tell you what threshold to use.

Stage 4 · Choose your threshold — 25 min

This stage carries the most marks.

  1. Write down two costs: \(c_{FN}\) for eating a poisonous mushroom, \(c_{FP}\) for discarding an edible one. Justify them in a sentence.
  2. Compute expected loss \(c_{FN}\!\cdot\!FN + c_{FP}\!\cdot\!FP\) across a grid of thresholds, and plot it.
  3. Where is the minimum? Compare it with \(c_{FP}/(c_{FP} + c_{FN})\).
  4. Report accuracy, mushrooms missed, and share of basket discarded at your chosen threshold and at 0.5.
  5. Say which error you chose to make more of, and why.

Your chosen rule will have worse accuracy than the \(t = 0.5\) rule. If that bothers you, say so in the write-up and then explain why you are doing it anyway.

Stage 5 · The graded outcome — 10 min

toxicity grades the same risk: edible, upset stomach, hospital, fatal.

  1. Fit an ordinal model with OrderedModel(..., distr="logit").
  2. What does the proportional-odds assumption claim about your dominant feature?
  3. Test it roughly: fit separate binary logits at each cut-point and compare the coefficients. Do they look like the same number?

If they diverge badly, the ordinal model is averaging effects that differ, and a multinomial model is the honest fallback — at the cost of giving up the ordering.

What you hand in

250 words, plus your script:

  • Your rule: the threshold, and the two costs that justify it
  • The majority-class baseline, and your accuracy beside it
  • The confusion matrix at your threshold and at 0.5
  • How many poisonous mushrooms your rule lets through, per hundred meals
  • The dominant feature, its odds ratio, and what it turns out to be
  • One thing this analysis does not license you to claim

And the standing rule: one output you identified as wrong, and how.

What loses marks

  • Reporting accuracy without the majority-class baseline
  • Using \(t = 0.5\) without noticing it is a choice
  • Choosing a threshold without writing the costs down
  • Describing an odds ratio as though it were a probability ratio
  • Reporting AUC as if it settled the decision
  • “The model is 88% accurate” as a headline, on data that is 71% one class

Two-minute report

One rule the threshold, and the costs behind it
One error the one you chose to make more of, and why
One number poisonous mushrooms per hundred meals
