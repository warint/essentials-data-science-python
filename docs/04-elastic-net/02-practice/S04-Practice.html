The Secret Recipe

Sixty bakes. Two hundred ingredients. Name the ones that matter.

The brief

A bakery has run 60 experimental bakes, recording grams of each of 200 candidate ingredients and a taste score. Only a handful of the ingredients actually do anything.

Deliverable: a named shortlist, with the sign and rough size of each ingredient’s effect, and an honest statement of which ones you are confident about and which you are not.

There is a planted list. Two of its members are a pair of near-identical ingredients, and one member is a signal your sample size cannot detect. Both of those facts are part of the answer.

Ninety minutes

15–30 Ridge and what it refuses to tell you
30–50 Lasso and the shortlist it gives you

Stage 1 · Watch OLS fail — 15 min

import essentials as ess
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler

r = ess.load("secret_recipe")
X = r.drop(columns=["bake_id", "taste_score"])
y = r.taste_score.to_numpy()
Xs = StandardScaler().fit_transform(X.values)
  1. Split into train and test. Fit LinearRegression on the training rows.
  2. Report training MSE and test MSE. Put them side by side.
  3. Compare test MSE with the variance of y. Which is bigger?
  4. Explain in one sentence why the training error came out where it did.

Do not skip step 3. A model that predicts worse than the mean of the outcome is worse than useless, and this one does.

Stage 2 · Ridge — 15 min

from sklearn.linear_model import RidgeCV
from sklearn.model_selection import KFold
import numpy as np

kf = KFold(10, shuffle=True, random_state=0)
ridge = RidgeCV(alphas=np.logspace(-2, 4, 25), cv=kf).fit(Xs, y)
  1. How many of the 200 coefficients are exactly zero?
  2. How many exceed 0.1 in absolute value?
  3. Report the cross-validated MSE.
  4. Now answer the client’s question: which ingredients matter? Say why ridge structurally cannot answer it.

Stage 3 · Lasso — 20 min

from sklearn.linear_model import LassoCV, lasso_path

lasso = LassoCV(cv=kf, random_state=0, max_iter=200_000).fit(Xs, y)
  1. How many ingredients did it select? Print them, sorted by absolute size.
  2. Report cross-validated MSE. Compare with ridge.
  3. Plot the coefficient path with lasso_path. Read it right to left, as the penalty relaxes. Which ingredients enter first?
  4. Is your selected set the right size? You do not know the truth yet, but you can reason about it: what is cross-validation optimising, and is that the same thing as picking the right variables?

Stage 4 · The two leaveners — 20 min

  1. Find the largest off-diagonal correlation in X. Which two ingredients?
  2. What did the lasso give each of them?
  3. Fit ElasticNetCV(l1_ratio=0.95, ...). What does it give each of them?
  4. Now the real question. Re-fit the elastic net with five different KFold random seeds. Print both coefficients and their sum each time. Which of the three numbers is stable?

Step 4 is what separates a group that has understood the elastic net from a group that has heard it is better. The answer changes what you are entitled to write down.

Stage 5 · What you cannot claim — 10 min

  1. Which of your selected ingredients would you actually report to the bakery, and which would you flag as uncertain?
  2. Your penalised coefficients are biased. In which direction, and why?
  3. Can you put a \(p\)-value on any of them? Explain.
  4. Is there an ingredient you suspect is real but cannot demonstrate? How would you find out?

Question 4 has an answer in this data, and finding it is worth more than any number of correctly selected variables.

What you hand in

250 words, plus your script:

  • Your shortlist, with signs and rough sizes
  • Training and test MSE for OLS, and one sentence on why
  • Cross-validated MSE for ridge, lasso and elastic net
  • The correlated pair, what each method did with it, and which quantity is identified
  • One ingredient you believe is real and cannot demonstrate, with your reasoning
  • Why you cannot attach \(p\)-values to any of this

And the standing rule: one output you identified as wrong, and how.

What loses marks

  • Fitting a penalised model on unstandardised columns
  • Reporting the lasso’s selected set as “the ingredients that matter”
  • Reporting penalised coefficients as effect sizes with no mention of shrinkage
  • Quoting \(p\)-values or stars on a cross-validated model
  • Saying the elastic net “found both” without checking whether the split is stable
  • Claiming the method missed nothing

Two-minute report

One shortlist with the uncertain members flagged
One pair and which quantity about it is identified
One absence a signal you think is there and cannot prove
