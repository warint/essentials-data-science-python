The Cat Café Effect

Did the cat work?

The brief

186 cafés, 12 quarters. Each adopts a resident cat in quarter 4, 7 or 10. The outcome is average customer dwell time in minutes.

Deliverable: an estimate of the effect of adopting a cat, with a design statement, an event study, and an explicit list of what your data cannot answer.

Every planted effect in this dataset is positive and growing. The estimator most people reach for first returns a significant negative number. You will get the wrong answer on purpose, and then work out why.

Ninety minutes

00–15 Get the wrong answer two-way fixed effects
15–40 Find out why Goodman-Bacon decomposition
40–60 Get the right answer group-time effects
60–75 The event study and how to read the pre-period
75–85 What you cannot know and why that matters
85–90 Write it up 250 words

Stage 1 · Get the wrong answer — 15 min

import essentials as ess
import statsmodels.formula.api as smf

d = ess.load("cat_cafe")
twfe = smf.ols("dwell_min ~ has_cat + C(cafe_id) + C(quarter)", data=d).fit()
  1. Plot mean dwell_min by quarter, one line per cohort. Does anything look wrong?
  2. Report the has_cat coefficient, its standard error, its \(t\) and its \(p\).
  3. Compare with ess.cat_cafe_truth(). What was planted?
  4. Write one sentence describing what a careless analyst would now publish.

Do step 1 before step 2. The raw data looks entirely reasonable, and that is part of the lesson — nothing in the plot warns you.

Stage 2 · Find out why — 25 min

import diff_diff as dd

bacon = dd.BaconDecomposition().fit(
    d, outcome="dwell_min", unit="cafe_id", time="quarter",
    first_treat="cat_arrival_quarter")
bacon.to_dataframe()
  1. Print the decomposition. How many 2×2 comparisons is TWFE averaging?
  2. Confirm the weighted sum reproduces the TWFE estimate. To how many decimals?
  3. Group the comparisons by type. What weight sits on each, and what is each type’s average estimate?
  4. Look at the later_vs_earlier rows. Which cafés are serving as the control group in those comparisons?
  5. Explain in three or four sentences why that produces a negative number when the true effects are positive and growing.

Step 5 is the single most important thing in this session. Write it out properly, in your own words — if you cannot, you do not have it yet.

Stage 3 · Get the right answer — 20 min

cs = dd.CallawaySantAnna(control_group="not_yet_treated").fit(
    d, outcome="dwell_min", unit="cafe_id", time="quarter",
    first_treat="cat_arrival_quarter", aggregate="group")

cs.overall_att
cs.group_time_effects        # a dict keyed by (cohort, quarter)
cs.group_effects
  1. Report the overall ATT. Compare its sign with TWFE’s.
  2. Build a table of every \(\text{ATT}(g,t)\) and merge it against ess.cat_cafe_truth().
  3. For the post-treatment cells: how many land within two standard errors of the planted value?
  4. Report the per-cohort aggregates against the planted averages.
  5. What does control_group="not_yet_treated" change, mechanically?

Stage 4 · The event study — 15 min

  1. Add an event_time column: quarter minus adoption quarter.
  2. Average the estimates by event_time and plot them with confidence intervals, with the planted truth overlaid.
  3. Are the pre-treatment estimates flat? Report their mean and the largest absolute value.
  4. How many of the placebo cells are individually significant at 5%? Now: how many would you expect, given how many tests you just ran?

Step 4 is a lesson about pre-trend tests generally. They are a multiple-testing problem, and reading them one star at a time will make you reject good designs.

Stage 5 · What you cannot know — 10 min

  1. Some post-treatment cells come back as NA. Which, and how many?
  2. Why? Count how many cafés are untreated in quarter 11.
  3. TWFE reported a number for those same quarters. Where did it get it?
  4. Compute a breakdown value: how large a differential trend, in minutes per quarter, would be needed to drive your estimate to zero? Compare it with the pre-treatment trend you can actually measure.
  5. Write the design recommendation: what should the cafés have done differently so this study could evaluate its own last phase?

Also worth trying, if you have time

  • dd.SunAbraham, dd.ImputationDiD (Borusyak–Jaravel–Spiess), or dd.TwoStageDiD (Gardner). Do they agree with Callaway–Sant’Anna? Report two estimators, not one.
  • control_group="never_treated" instead. What happens, and why?
  • Add covariates=["seats", "wifi_mbps"] to the CS fit. Should it change anything? Does it?

What you hand in

250 words, plus your script:

  • The design, first: who is treated, when, and what serves as control
  • The TWFE estimate, and the decomposition showing where it came from
  • The weight on forbidden comparisons, and their average estimate
  • Your group-time estimates against the planted truth
  • The event study, with the pre-period read as a set of tests
  • What is not identified, explicitly, and why
  • A breakdown value, not an assertion that trends look parallel

And the standing rule: one output you identified as wrong, and how. This session hands you one on a plate.

What loses marks

  • Reporting the TWFE estimate as the answer
  • Reporting the right answer without explaining the wrong one
  • “The parallel trends assumption holds” — it is untestable; bound it instead
  • Reading twelve placebo tests one star at a time
  • Treating the NA cells as a software problem
  • An event study with no pre-treatment periods shown
  • Any causal verb attached to a specification you have not decomposed

Two-minute report

Two numbers the wrong one and the right one, with the reason
One weight the share on forbidden comparisons
One limit what your data cannot identify, and what to do about it

And congratulations: you now have a toolkit that runs from a straight line to an estimator that knows the limits of its own identification. That last property is rarer than it should be.
